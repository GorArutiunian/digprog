# Part A — Foundations

## Ch 0 — How to use DigProg

**Core claim:** DigProg is a loop (video → quiz → one real-world action), not a binge library.

**Broader discussion:** Most beginners fail by consuming. They feel productive while avoiding the scary part: talking to humans and asking for money. DigProg deliberately keeps videos short so the “course” cannot become a hiding place. The book holds the why and the worksheet; the video shows the move; the quiz forces retrieval; the attachment forces an artifact you can reuse.

**Micro-example:** Watch L001 (customer/offer/delivery), answer the quiz, then write one sentence about *your* idea using the same three parts.

**Caution:** If you “finish” videos without conversations, you did not finish DigProg.

**24h task:** Block 45 minutes tomorrow. Open Module 01. Do one action outside the screen.

**Open for final:** Exact onboarding email sequence + waitlist-to-student path.

## Ch 1 — What is a business?

**Core claim:** A business provides something customers value in exchange for payment. Start with customer → offer → delivery.

**Broader discussion:** People confuse *activity* with *business*. Building an app friends love is valuable learning, but praise is interest, not paying demand. DigProg does not claim “no revenue means it is not a business” as a philosophy fight — it claims that **for learning and progress**, completed purchases are stronger evidence than compliments. Payment can happen before or after delivery depending on the arrangement; delivery is still a required part of the promise.

**Micro-example:** Maya sells a $79 invoicing guide to freelance designers and delivers a download link. Customer = designers. Offer = guide at $79. Delivery = download.

**Caution:** One sale does not prove long-term success. It proves someone paid once.

**24h task:** Write: “I help ___ do ___ so they can ___.” Then label customer / offer / delivery.

**Open for final:** Visual diagram pages + more contrast cases (service, hybrid).

## Ch 2 — Money words (no MBA)

**Core claim:** Revenue, cost, profit, and cash are different; beginners mix them and make bad decisions.

**Broader discussion:** You can show “profit” on paper and still be broke if cash is delayed. Price is what the buyer pays; margin is what remains after unit cost. DigProg uses rough numbers early because precision theater delays first sales. Later, when money is real, tighten the model.

**Micro-example:** Guide price $79; tools $3; your time 2 hours. If your time is worth $25/hour to you, “true” cost includes that — even if cash cost is $3.

**Caution:** Vanity metrics (likes, empty waitlists) are not cash.

**24h task:** List costs to deliver one unit of a simple digital product.

## Ch 3 — Are you an entrepreneur?

**Core claim:** Entrepreneurship is a behavior pattern under uncertainty, not a personality cult.

**Broader discussion:** Useful traits: curiosity, persistence, talking to strangers, finishing small loops. Harmful if unchecked: perfectionism, endless research, fear of selling. Intrapreneurship (building inside a company) is valid. DigProg works for side paths and full jumps; the constraint is hours and kill criteria, not identity.

**24h task:** Score yourself 1–5 on: ship ugly v1; ask for money; change with evidence; sustain 5–10h/week for 30 days.

## Ch 4 — When to start

**Core claim:** You need minimum viable readiness, not total readiness.

**Broader discussion:** Need: a problem you understand, a way to reach ~20 people, an offer draft, and 5–10 honest hours/week. Don’t need: LLC day one, logo, perfect site, ads budget. Keep survival money if risk is high. Set kill criteria so you don’t romanticize a dead idea.

**24h task:** Write start date + kill date (14 or 30 days).

# Part B — Build the offer

## Ch 5 — Inventory what you have

**Core claim:** Start from skills, time, and reachable people — not from fantasy markets.

**Broader discussion:** Beginners invent “AI for everyone” because it sounds big. DigProg prefers unfair advantages you already have: language, niche knowledge, a community, a craft. Time budgets must be truthful. Free tools first.

**24h task:** Asset list — skills / audience / tools / constraints.

## Ch 6 — Pick one idea

**Core claim:** Dump many ideas, kill most, commit to one for 14 days.

**Broader discussion:** Kill ideas with no urgency, no reachable buyers, legal/ToS death, or $10k required before learning. Keep ideas you can deliver in under 7 days. The one-page offer (who / problem / promise / how / price / next step) is the artifact that makes the idea discussable.

**24h task:** Fill one-page offer draft.

## Ch 7 — Price without panic

**Core claim:** Price before the product is perfect; underpricing often hides fear of sales.

**Broader discussion:** A ladder (Starter/Builder/Operator) can come later. First, one clear price you can say out loud. Refund rules should be honorable and survivable. Test: would you pay this for the outcome if you had the problem?

**24h task:** Set a test price + refund rule.

## Ch 8 — Numbers that decide “will it work?”

**Core claim:** Rough unit economics beat vibes.

**Broader discussion:** Estimate cost to deliver, time per sale, and how many sales cover a week of effort. Time-to-first-dollar for DigProg-style digital offers should aim measured in days/weeks, not “someday.” Track conversations, offers, closes, refunds.

**24h task:** Rough break-even for your offer.

# Part C — Market & clients

## Ch 9 — Validation in 48 hours

**Core claim:** Talk to humans before building for months.

**Broader discussion:** Five problem interviews beat fifty survey likes. Ask about the last time the problem happened, what they tried, and what they would pay. Polite interest ≠ buying signal. Smoke tests: short landing + CTA + outbound.

**24h task:** Message five people with interview questions (not a pitch).

## Ch 10 — Where clients come from

**Core claim:** Warm network first, then outbound, then inbound content.

**Broader discussion:** DigProg is not “become an influencer first.” Reach people you can already message. Use a sheet CRM. Follow up three times. Most sales die from silence, not from “bad offers.”

**24h task:** List 20 reachable people/places; send 5 messages.

# Part D — Sales

## Ch 11 — Sales is not sleaze

**Core claim:** Sales helps someone decide whether the offer solves a real pain.

**Broader discussion:** Discovery before pitch. Pain → impact → urgency → offer. Scripts are training wheels. Guarantees only what you can deliver. DigProg rejects fake scarcity and fake testimonials.

**24h task:** Write five discovery questions + one closing line.

## Ch 12 — Objection map

**Core claim:** Objections are information, not insults.

**Broader discussion:** Map no money / no time / need to think / competitor / DIY. Each has a honest response path (smaller plan, async delivery, specific follow-up, clear difference, cost of delay).

**24h task:** Roleplay three objections.

## Ch 13 — Close & deliver

**Core claim:** Closing is a clear ask; delivery is the brand.

**Broader discussion:** Soft vs hard close. After payment, over-deliver in 48 hours. Ask for a testimonial after the first win. Document delivery so it becomes a system.

**24h task:** Write a welcome message template.

---

# Part E — Distribution & systems

## Ch 14 — Content that sells

**Core claim:** One painful question per short; batch production; cadence over virality fantasy.

**Broader discussion:** DigProg’s own growth engine can be UGC-style clips from lesson ideas — but the business still needs sales conversations. Content supports distribution; it does not replace offers and delivery.

**24h task:** Write seven hooks for your offer.

## Ch 15 — Systems for a team of three (or a disciplined solo)

**Core claim:** Roles and scoreboards prevent chaos.

**Broader discussion:** Weekly scoreboard: money, conversations, lessons shipped, posts. Free stack first. Automate only after the process works manually.

**24h task:** Assign roles or calendar blocks.

## Ch 16 — Capstone: ship

**Core claim:** Graduation = shipped artifacts + outreach + delivery, not “watched everything.”

**Checklist:** one-page offer · price + refund · delivery folder · 10 outreaches · CRM · week-2 plan.
