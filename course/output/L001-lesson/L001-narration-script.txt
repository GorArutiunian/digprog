DigProg · L01 — What a business is
Voice: en-US-BrianNeural · rate +15%

You built an app. Your friends love it. Does that mean customers will pay? Not yet - you have evidence of interest, but not of paying demand.

A business provides something customers value in exchange for payment. To understand how it works, start with three parts: customer, offer, and delivery.

The customer is the person or organization buying. Ask: who are they, and what do they need?

The offer is what they can buy: a clear product or service, its price, and what is included.

Delivery is how they receive what you promised. Payment can happen before or after delivery, depending on the arrangement.

For example, Maya sells a seventy-nine-dollar invoicing guide to freelance designers.

Her customers are freelance designers who need help organizing invoices. Her offer is the guide for seventy-nine dollars. She delivers it through a download link.

Now compare that with an app that friends praise but nobody buys. The feedback may be useful. It does not yet show that customers will pay. A purchase gives stronger evidence of paying demand, although one sale does not prove long-term success.

When you examine an idea, ask: who is the customer, what is the offer, and how will it be delivered?
